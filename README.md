# HELIOS_TechX
HELIOS VAULT

Intelligent Adaptive Mobility for Autonomous Hoppers

HELIOS is a robotics research project exploring adaptive mobility for autonomous robots operating on uneven and challenging terrain. VAULT focuses on evaluating when conventional rolling becomes ineffective and when alternative movement strategies, such as hopping, may improve mobility.

Overview

Traditional wheeled robots can struggle with loose surfaces, obstacles, and uneven terrain. HELIOS explores an adaptive approach in which a robot monitors its movement, evaluates terrain conditions, and selects an appropriate mobility strategy.

The system follows a continuous feedback loop:

Sense → Model → Predict → Decide → Act → Verify

Key Features

- Adaptive Mobility: Explores rolling, hopping, and alternative movement strategies.
- Terrain Awareness: Uses sensor inputs and environmental parameters to assess mobility conditions.
- Predictive Modelling: Evaluates potential movement outcomes before selecting an action.
- Feedback-Driven Control: Monitors movement outcomes and updates subsequent decisions.
- Simulation-Based Research: Enables controlled experiments to compare mobility strategies.
- Energy-Aware Decisions: Investigates the trade-off between movement efficiency and terrain traversal.

System Architecture

1. Sensing: Collects motion, orientation, and other relevant sensor data.
2. State Estimation: Estimates the robot's current movement and terrain interaction.
3. Prediction: Evaluates possible mobility outcomes.
4. Decision-Making: Selects a suitable movement strategy based on the estimated conditions.
5. Execution: Simulates or commands the selected action.
6. Verification: Evaluates the result and feeds the observations back into the system.

Research Objectives

- Investigate the limitations of conventional rolling on difficult terrain.
- Explore when hopping may offer an advantage over continuous rolling.
- Evaluate mobility, energy consumption, and traversal success.
- Study uncertainty in terrain assessment and movement prediction.
- Develop a foundation for autonomous, adaptive robotic mobility.

Technology

The implementation may use robotics simulation, 3D visualisation, physics-based modelling, and data-driven decision-making. Specific tools and technologies will be documented as the prototype develops.

Current Status

Stage: Research and prototype development.

HELIOS VAULT is an experimental project. Its performance, physical feasibility, and advantages over baseline mobility strategies require systematic testing and validation.

Future Work

- Develop and refine the interactive mobility simulation.
- Implement baseline and adaptive controllers.
- Introduce configurable terrain types and obstacles.
- Compare traversal success, energy use, and recovery behaviour.
- Investigate uncertainty-aware decision-making.
- Explore eventual validation on a physical robotic platform.

Vision

To advance autonomous robotics by enabling machines to recognise mobility limitations, adapt their movement, and navigate challenging environments more intelligently.
